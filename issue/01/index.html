The bipartisan waterfowl is expected to honk aggressively during the next fiscal quarter, sparking fears of a potential “beak-down” in negotiations.

Washington D.C. —

In a stunning 3 a.m. vote that witnesses described as “completely silent and somehow louder than anything I’ve ever heard,” the House of Representatives voted 294–141 to officially abandon the statutory debt limit and replace it with a 40-pound Canada Goose, designated Henrietta, who will henceforth be released into the Capitol Rotunda upon the commencement of any budget-related proceedings.

The legislation, known formally as the Avian Fiscal Responsibility and Emotional Deterrence Act of 2026, passed with rare bipartisan support. Representatives from both sides of the aisle cited a shared desire to engage with an adversary who, unlike their colleagues, “at least has a discernible emotional state and a consistent set of values.”

“Henrietta doesn’t need to be briefed,” said a senior appropriations staffer who agreed to speak only from inside a supply closet. “She already knows. They all know. That’s what they don’t want you to understand.”

Plinth called the transition “orderly, necessary, and in keeping with our long-standing commitment to institutional norms.” He declined to elaborate on whether Henrietta had been briefed on existing debt obligations, adding only that she had “reviewed the relevant documents” and that “her position was clear.”

“Henrietta has already rejected the discretionary spending bill, denied two committee requests, and is nesting on the Ways and Means Chairman’s jacket.”

The Congressional Budget Office released a preliminary estimate suggesting that the average honk from Henrietta produces approximately $1.2 trillion in suppressed anxiety among members present, making her “technically more fiscally effective than the previous mechanism, and considerably more honest.”

Senate Minority Leader Eugenia T. Barns declined to comment, citing what her office described as “active goose proximity concerns.” A spokesperson confirmed that Senator Barns had “reviewed the goose briefing” and would “circle back pending further study of the goose.”

As of press time, Henrietta had already rejected the discretionary spending bill, denied two committee requests, and was nesting on the Ways and Means Committee Chairman’s jacket. The jacket was unavailable for comment. —N.C.

Supreme Court Rules in Favor of Guy Who Screams at Pigeons
By Lt. Col. (Ret.) Nancy Prune, Legal Affairs

Washington D.C. — The Supreme Court ruled 7–2 Thursday in Grabowski v. City of Mercer that Raymond T. Grabowski, 54, known to local residents as “that one guy at the park,” has an inviolable constitutional right to stand in the middle of Clement Park and scream at pigeons for any reason or no reason at all, without permit, license, or advance notice to the city.

Writing for the majority, Justice Aldous T. Pemberton declared that Mr. Grabowski’s practice of standing near the concrete pavilion and shouting “You know what you did” and “I know it was you specifically” at a group of approximately thirty pigeons constitutes protected speech under the First Amendment and, in an unusual footnote, “possibly protected prophecy under the Third.”

Justice Clarissa R. Holmsworth, in dissent, wrote that the majority had “failed to grapple with the fundamental question of whether the pigeons have standing,” adding that she had “reviewed the pigeons’ petition” and found it “surprisingly well-organized.”

Mr. Grabowski declined to speak to reporters but gestured meaningfully at a pigeon. —N.C.
